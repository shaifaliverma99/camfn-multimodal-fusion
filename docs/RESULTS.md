# Real Results — CAMFN on Open Substitute Data

Generated by `experiments/run_real_experiments.py`, run for 3 seeds
(42, 43, 44) — full per-seed logs and CSVs in `experiments/results/`,
aggregate in `experiments/results/aggregate_summary.json`. Seed 42's
checkpoints (with `--save-checkpoints`) are what the dashboard loads.

**Nothing below is simulated.** Every number is a real held-out metric
from an actual training run on the real, open datasets described in
`DATA_SOURCES.md`. Small datasets (PD motor drawings, n=102; PD voice,
n=195) are evaluated with cross-validation (5-fold stratified / 5-fold
subject-grouped) so every real sample is scored exactly once, rather than
reporting a single lucky/unlucky 30-sample split.

## Per-modality baselines (mean ± population std over 3 seeds)

| Task | Eval | n | Accuracy | Macro-F1 | AUC-ROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AD vs HC (MRI) | held-out split | 1800 | 0.890 ± 0.037 | 0.803 ± 0.101 | 0.965 ± 0.011 |
| PD vs healthy (spiral drawing) | 5-fold CV | 102 | 0.807 ± 0.032 | 0.806 ± 0.033 | 0.887 ± 0.023 |
| PD vs healthy (wave drawing) | 5-fold CV | 102 | 0.765 ± 0.021 | 0.762 ± 0.023 | 0.862 ± 0.033 |
| PD vs healthy (voice) | 5-fold subject-grouped CV | 195 | 0.749 ± 0.026 | 0.583 ± 0.003 | 0.527 ± 0.037 |
| Seizure vs non-seizure (EEG) | held-out split | 1725 | 0.988 ± 0.001 | 0.981 ± 0.001 | 0.999 ± 0.0002 |

**The voice model is weak, not (as an earlier single-split evaluation
suggested) completely broken.** Evaluating it via 5-fold *subject-grouped*
cross-validation over the full 195 recordings — instead of one 30-sample
held-out split — gives a materially different and more trustworthy
picture: macro-F1 0.58 (real signal, well above the ~0.33 floor of
always guessing one class, though AUC 0.53 is barely above chance) rather
than the complete majority-class collapse (macro-F1 0.44, AUC 0.54, 0%
healthy-class recall) that the original single 30-sample split showed.
The lesson we're keeping in the paper either way: **a single small
held-out split can make a model look either much better or much worse
than it is** — this is exactly why we moved to CV for both small PD
datasets.

## Joint CAMFN experiment vs. a trivial fusion ablation

Both trained/evaluated on the *identical* 70/15/15 split of the combined
4-cohort dataset, same seeds — so any difference is attributable to the
fusion mechanism, not the data.

| Metric | CAMFN (cross-attn + gated fusion) | Ablation (masked mean, no attention/gating) |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | 0.747 ± 0.128 | **0.853 ± 0.037** |
| Macro-F1 | 0.546 ± 0.022 | 0.581 ± 0.068 |
| AUC-ROC (OvR macro) | **0.942 ± 0.035** | 0.924 ± 0.007 |
| Sensitivity — AD (minority class) | 0.387 ± 0.438 | 0.265 ± 0.342 |

n_test = 2966 for both. **This is a real negative result for CAMFN's
core architectural claim, and we report it as such**: replacing the
pairwise cross-attention + `ModalityTokenTransformer` +
`DynamicGatedFusion` stack with a fixed masked mean of the same encoders'
embeddings gets *higher and far more stable* accuracy (std 0.037 vs
0.128) and comparable macro-F1, at a fraction of the parameters and
compute. CAMFN's ranking quality (AUC) is marginally better, but its
operating-point performance is worse and much less reproducible across
seeds. Our reading: cross-attention is built to exploit genuine
cross-modal correlation, and in this experiment there isn't any (every
sample has exactly one real modality — see below) — so the extra
machinery adds optimization variance without a signal to fuse, while the
trivial baseline is both simpler and, empirically, better-behaved here.
This is exactly the outcome you'd predict from the honest framing below;
we would not expect this result to hold on data where multiple real
modalities are genuinely present per patient.

## Honest framing (read this before citing any of these numbers)

1. **No sample has more than one real modality.** OASIS-1, Parkinson's
   drawings, UCI voice, and the EEG sources are four independent cohorts
   with no shared patients. The joint experiment (and the ablation above)
   tests whether a shared architecture can route single-modality inputs
   correctly and whether a complex fusion mechanism helps when there's no
   real cross-modal signal to fuse — not whether fusing multiple real
   modalities from one patient improves diagnosis, since that never
   happens in this data.
2. **The AD/PD baselines use 2D image slices/drawings**, not the
   volumetric MRI or DaT-SPECT that ADNI/PPMI would provide.
3. **PD motor CV folds are still small** (~20 test samples/fold) even
   though the full n=102/195 is now evaluated across folds — treat these
   as directional, not high-precision estimates.
4. **The Epilepsy and AD-vs-HC results are the most trustworthy** numbers
   here: large test sets (1725, 1800), low run-to-run variance.
5. Re-run `python experiments/run_real_experiments.py --seed <N>
   [--save-checkpoints]` to reproduce or extend any of this.
