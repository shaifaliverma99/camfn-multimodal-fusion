# Real Results — CAMFN on Open Substitute Data (Subject-Leakage-Corrected)

Generated by `experiments/run_real_experiments.py`, run for 3 seeds
(42, 43, 44) — full per-seed logs/CSVs in `experiments/results/`,
aggregate in `experiments/results/aggregate_summary.json`. Seed 42's
checkpoints and raw predictions (`--save-checkpoints --save-predictions`)
back both the dashboard and `paper/figures/`.

**Nothing below is simulated.** Every number is a real held-out metric
from an actual training run on the real, open datasets described in
`DATA_SOURCES.md`.

## A real leakage bug we found and fixed — read this first

An earlier version of this experiment split the Alzheimer MRI slices and
the Parkinson's drawings with a **plain random/stratified split over
individual files**, not grouped by patient. Both datasets have many
samples per subject:

- **OASIS-1 MRI**: 86,437 slices from only **347 unique subjects**
  (~249 slices/subject). A random split puts many of the same patient's
  slices in both train and test.
- **Parkinson's drawings**: the dataset's *own* provided training/testing
  folders are not subject-disjoint either — the same subject IDs (e.g.
  `V01`-`V11`) appear on both sides, for both the spiral and wave tasks,
  in both classes.

That leakage let the model partly memorize patient-specific
anatomy/artifacts instead of learning disease signal, and it inflated
accuracy. We caught this, fixed the split (subject-grouped
`GroupShuffleSplit`/`GroupKFold` for both the outer train/test/CV split
*and* the inner train/val carve-out — see `src/data/mri_dataset.py`,
`src/data/motor_dataset.py`, and `experiments/run_real_experiments.py`),
and re-ran everything. The corrected numbers below are **substantially
lower** than what the leaky split reported — that drop is the leakage
being removed, not a regression:

| Task | Leaky split (before) | Subject-grouped (after, corrected) |
|---|---|---|
| AD vs HC (MRI) accuracy | ~0.89–0.90 | **0.757 ± 0.058** |
| AD vs HC macro-F1 | ~0.80–0.85 | **0.549 ± 0.088** |
| PD spiral accuracy | ~0.81 | **0.690 ± 0.047** |
| PD wave accuracy | ~0.77 | **0.677 ± 0.084** |

The Epilepsy and voice results barely moved (EEG had no recoverable
subject grouping to begin with; voice was already subject-grouped in the
prior pass) — consistent with the leakage being specific to the two
datasets where it was actually present.

## Per-modality baselines (mean ± population std over 3 seeds, corrected)

| Task | Eval | n | Accuracy | Macro-F1 | AUC-ROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AD vs HC (MRI) | subject-grouped split | 1896 | 0.757 ± 0.058 | 0.549 ± 0.088 | 0.840 ± 0.010 |
| PD vs healthy (spiral) | 5-fold subject-grouped CV | 102 | 0.690 ± 0.047 | 0.680 ± 0.054 | 0.759 ± 0.063 |
| PD vs healthy (wave) | 5-fold subject-grouped CV | 102 | 0.677 ± 0.084 | 0.662 ± 0.092 | 0.691 ± 0.071 |
| PD vs healthy (voice) | 5-fold subject-grouped CV | 195 | 0.739 ± 0.023 | 0.576 ± 0.024 | 0.508 ± 0.029 |
| Seizure vs non-seizure (EEG) | held-out split | 1725 | 0.988 ± 0.002 | 0.982 ± 0.003 | 0.999 ± 0.0003 |

**AD sensitivity (the minority "AD" class) is only 0.183 ± 0.151** —
once leakage is removed, the model is much better at recognizing HC
(sensitivity 0.965) than AD, i.e. it's biased toward the majority class.
This is a materially different, more honest picture than the earlier
(leaky) result suggested, and a real limitation to disclose, not paper
over.

**Voice remains weak** (AUC 0.508 — indistinguishable from chance) even
under proper subject-grouped CV; this is consistent across both the
pre- and post-leakage-fix evaluations and reflects real data scarcity
(195 recordings, ~31 subjects), not an evaluation artifact.

## Joint CAMFN experiment vs. a trivial fusion ablation

Both trained/evaluated on the *identical*, now subject-grouped-per-cohort
70/15/15 split of the combined 4-cohort dataset, same seeds.

| Metric | CAMFN (cross-attn + gated fusion) | Ablation (masked mean, no attention/gating) |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | 0.659 ± 0.191 | **0.802 ± 0.055** |
| Macro-F1 | 0.504 ± 0.055 | **0.553 ± 0.018** |
| AUC-ROC (OvR macro) | 0.886 ± 0.029 | **0.929 ± 0.028** |
| Sensitivity — AD (minority class) | 0.333 ± 0.471 | 0.057 ± 0.081 |

n_test ≈ 3021 for both. **The ablation now wins on every metric we
report, and is far more stable** (accuracy std 0.191 vs 0.055). This is
a stronger version of the same finding from before the leakage fix: under
conditions where no sample has more than one real modality, CAMFN's
cross-attention + dynamic gating does not earn its added complexity
relative to a fixed masked mean. We are not aware of an explanation for
this other than the one we already gave: there is no real cross-modal
structure in this data for the attention mechanism to exploit, and the
extra parameters/optimization surface add variance without a
corresponding benefit.

## Honest framing (read this before citing any of these numbers)

1. **No sample has more than one real modality.** OASIS-1, Parkinson's
   drawings, UCI voice, and the EEG sources are four independent cohorts
   with no shared patients. The joint experiment and ablation test
   whether a shared architecture can route single-modality inputs
   correctly, and whether a complex fusion mechanism helps when there is
   no real cross-modal signal — not whether fusing multiple real
   modalities from one patient improves diagnosis.
2. **The AD/PD baselines use 2D image slices/drawings**, not the
   volumetric MRI or DaT-SPECT that ADNI/PPMI provide.
3. **Sample sizes remain small for PD tasks** (n=102/102/195) even with
   full-sample cross-validation — treat these as directional.
4. **Epilepsy is the most trustworthy result** here: a large test set
   (n=1725), essentially unaffected by the leakage fix (no recoverable
   subject ID in this corpus to have leaked in the first place), and
   consistent with the broader CHB-MIT/EEG literature's reported ranges.
5. **No hyperparameter search was performed anywhere** — epochs,
   batch size, and learning rate are fixed by hand per experiment, not
   tuned against a validation set. None of these numbers should be read
   as a ceiling on what these architectures could achieve.
6. Re-run `python experiments/run_real_experiments.py --seed <N>
   [--save-checkpoints] [--save-predictions]` to reproduce or extend any
   of this.
